Commercial Litigation Focused on the Business Objective

Neil L. Postrygacz represents businesses, executives, entrepreneurs and investors in sophisticated commercial disputes in New York state and federal courts, as well as in arbitration and mediation. The representation is direct and senior-level, from the initial assessment of a dispute through negotiation, motion practice, discovery, hearings and trial.

Commercial disputes rarely fit neatly into one category. A disagreement that begins as a contract claim may also involve allegations of fraud, breach of fiduciary duty, misuse of confidential information, ownership rights, interference with business relationships or a request for emergency relief. The litigation strategy should account for the complete business problem, not only the pleadings.

Core objective:

Identify the legal and factual pressure points early, preserve leverage, control avoidable cost and pursue the result that best protects the client's business interests.

State Court, Federal Court, Arbitration and Mediation

The proper forum can materially affect a commercial case. Jurisdiction, venue, contractual forum-selection provisions, arbitration clauses, procedural rules and available provisional remedies all matter at the outset.

Neil handles matters in New York state and federal courts and in arbitral proceedings, and also uses mediation and direct negotiation where those routes can advance the client's objective. The approach is not litigation for its own sake. It is to choose and execute the process most likely to produce a commercially sensible result.

New York Commercial Division

When applicable, complex New York business disputes may proceed in the Commercial Division of the New York State Supreme Court. Commercial Division practice involves specialized rules and procedures designed for substantial business cases, making early attention to forum, case management, disclosure and motion strategy particularly important.

Strategy Before the Complaint Is Filed

Many of the most important litigation decisions occur before filing. Early assessment can shape the available remedies, the evidentiary record and the opposing party's incentives.

  • Evaluate claims, defenses and contractual remedies.
  • Identify critical documents, communications and witnesses.
  • Assess jurisdiction, venue, arbitration provisions and governing law.
  • Determine whether emergency injunctive relief may be necessary.
  • Analyze damages, business interruption and practical settlement ranges.
  • Preserve electronically stored information and other evidence.
  • Plan discovery around the issues most likely to change leverage or outcome.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should a business bring litigation counsel into a dispute?

Usually before positions harden or evidence is lost. Early involvement can help preserve documents, evaluate contractual notice requirements, identify emergency remedies and shape communications so that business negotiations and litigation strategy work together rather than against each other.

Does every commercial dispute need to be litigated through trial?

No. Many disputes resolve through negotiation, mediation, motion practice or a negotiated business solution. The appropriate route depends on the claims, evidence, economics, timing and the client's objectives.

Can the firm handle disputes that involve technology or international parties?

Yes. The firm's representative matters include disputes involving digital assets, telecommunications, trade secrets and cross-border discovery for use in foreign litigation.
